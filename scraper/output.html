Texas measles outbreak

A second child in Texas has died from measles complications amid an ongoing outbreak, with 481 cases reported statewide. The unvaccinated school-aged child had no known underlying health conditions, according to UMC Health System. The outbreak, spanning Texas, New Mexico, Oklahoma, and Kansas, has reached 569 cases, primarily among unvaccinated minors. HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. visited affected communities, emphasizing the MMR vaccine's importance, though his response faced criticism for downplaying the crisis. Health experts warn of underreported cases and rising hospitalizations, particularly among young children at higher risk. Over 70% of Texas cases involve children and teens, with Gaines County as the epicenter. New Mexico and Oklahoma reported 54 and 10 cases, respectively. Dr. Paul Offit criticized Kennedy’s handling, linking the outbreak to vaccine misinformation. US Sen. Bill Cassidy urged unequivocal pro-vaccine messaging to prevent further deaths. Texas health departments and the CDC are collaborating to contain the spread, offering drive-up screenings in Lubbock County.

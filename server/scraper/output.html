US-China Trade War Escalates Amid Communication Standoff

The US and China are entrenched in a trade conflict, with reciprocal tariff hikes—the US imposing 145% on Chinese goods and China responding with 125%. The Trump administration insists China must initiate high-level talks, but Beijing demands dialogue on equal terms. Backchannel efforts, including through Elon Musk, failed due to disputes over trusted intermediaries. China considered targeting US firms like Apple and Tesla but hesitated over consumer backlash risks. Experts warn of potential escalations, such as China restricting rare earth exports or selling US Treasuries, which could cause mutual economic harm. Trump seeks a broader deal addressing trade imbalances, fentanyl, and TikTok, though past agreements underdelivered. Internal US divisions persist, with adviser Peter Navarro dismissing China’s leverage, while others predict "mutual assured destruction." Xi Jinping’s strengthened domestic position allows prolonged resistance, framing the conflict as resistance to US suppression. The stalemate risks global economic disruption, with tensions spilling into tech and geopolitics, particularly regarding Taiwan. Neither side shows willingness to concede, prolonging uncertainty.

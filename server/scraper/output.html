South Korea rape case

Choi Mal-ja, 78, is fighting to clear her name after being wrongfully convicted in 1965 for defending herself against an attempted rape. At 18, she bit off part of her attacker’s tongue during an assault, yet was prosecuted and given a harsher sentence than her assailant. Her retrial, granted by the Supreme Court in 2023, challenges patriarchal legal norms that historically dismissed sexual violence. The case exposes systemic injustices, including invasive virginity tests and societal pressure to marry her attacker. A successful retrial could redefine self-defense laws, setting a precedent for future sexual violence cases in South Korea. Despite progress from movements like #MeToo, challenges persist, as seen in a 2017 case where a woman was similarly convicted. Over 22,000 sexual assaults were reported in 2023, yet victims’ self-defense claims often remain unrecognized. Supported by women’s groups and a 15,000-signature petition, Choi’s decades-long struggle highlights ongoing efforts to reform legal and societal attitudes toward gender justice.

UK Supreme Court ruling

The UK Supreme Court unanimously ruled that the legal definition of "woman" under the Equality Act 2010 refers to biological sex, excluding transgender women even with a gender recognition certificate (GRC). The case, brought by For Women Scotland, centered on whether trans women should have the same protections as biological women in equality laws. The court argued that interpreting "sex" as certificated gender would create legal inconsistencies, particularly affecting single-sex spaces like changing rooms and shelters. While the ruling was celebrated by gender-critical groups as affirming biological reality, LGBTQ+ advocates warned it undermines transgender rights. The decision intensifies debates over transgender inclusion in the UK, where hate crimes based on sexual identity rose 112% in 2023. Amnesty International and Stonewall expressed concern, noting trans people remain protected under gender reassignment discrimination clauses. The government endorsed the ruling, emphasizing protection of single-sex spaces. Critics, including trans advocate Ella Morgan, fear increased marginalization and safety risks for the community.

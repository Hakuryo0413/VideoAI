US-China Relations

The US and China escalated their trade war, with the US imposing 145% tariffs on Chinese goods and China retaliating with 125% tariffs. Both sides refuse to initiate high-level talks, each demanding the other act first. President Xi Jinping emphasized China’s self-reliance, while the Trump administration insists Beijing must make the first move. Communication remains stalled due to protocol disputes and distrust, with failed backchannel attempts through figures like Elon Musk. The conflict risks expanding to non-tariff measures, including rare earth mineral bans or US Treasury bond sales, potentially causing mutual economic harm. Previous agreements, such as China’s unmet purchase commitments, and issues like fentanyl exports and TikTok’s restructuring remain unresolved. Experts warn of a "mutually assured destruction" scenario, with neither side backing down. Political posturing complicates resolution efforts, as both leaders avoid appearing weak. The standoff highlights deepening strategic rivalry, with implications for global trade and technology sectors.

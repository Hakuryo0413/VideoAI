**North Macedonia Nightclub Fire**  
At least 59 people, mostly aged 16–24, died and 155 were injured in a fire at the Pulse nightclub in Kochani, North Macedonia, on August 27. The blaze, sparked by pyrotechnics during a performance by band DNK, ignited flammable ceiling materials, triggering sprinklers and thick smoke. Many victims were trampled in the ensuing chaos. Interior Minister Panche Toshkovski confirmed the cause, noting 20 suspects were identified, with 15 detained, including the club owner. Among the dead was footballer Andrej Lazarov, 25. Hospitals reported treating severe burns and injuries, with 27 critically injured sent abroad. Prime Minister Hristijan Mickoski declared a national mourning week, calling the loss “irreparable.” International leaders, including EU officials and Pope Francis, offered condolences. The tragedy echoes past nightclub fires in Romania (2015, 64 deaths) and Brazil (2013, 240 deaths), underscoring safety concerns.  

**China’s ‘Made in Russia’ Craze**  
Chinese pop-up stores selling Russian goods have surged since 2022, fueled by strengthened Sino-Russian ties post-Ukraine invasion. Over 2,500 new companies, mostly in Heilongjiang, now trade Russian chocolates, vodka, and honey, though many products are falsely labeled. Investigations revealed items like sausages and milk powder were domestically produced but marketed as Russian. State media dubbed the trend “crazy,” while regulators cracked down on misleading ads, closing or fining stores in Shanghai and elsewhere. Despite authenticity issues, demand persists, driven by favorable Chinese views of Russia (66% positive in a Tsinghua poll). The Russian Export Center plans 300 official stores by 2024, promoting genuine products with a “Made in Russia” dove logo. E-commerce boomed after the 2022 “Russian State Pavilion” viral launch, with $2.3 million in sales at a Shenyang fair. While cultural affinity and political alignment bolster the trend, scrutiny over counterfeit goods continues.
